WHY THE GAP GROWS

Costs, revenue and project choices.

Personnel & operations

About 93% of added net spending after FY2029 comes from personnel and operations in this scenario. Extending historical growth is not a prediction or an inflation-only estimate.

Slower revenue growth

Property-tax receipts grow 2% a year after FY2029; most other revenue stays flat. These are scenario assumptions.

Projects & debt

Cash contributions and loan payments are already in spending. A project budget is not a one-year cost or an amount that can automatically be cut.

Net spending and set-asides are shown in nominal dollars. Set-asides move money into project or reserve funds. No matched-period inflation benchmark has been verified.

THEN / MOVE FROM CONCERN TO OPTIONS

How do we close the gap?

Without lower spending or enough growth in other revenue, closing the projected gap would require higher property taxes.

If that is not the outcome you want, help identify the alternatives—and the tradeoffs.

Explore the options ↓
01Spend more efficientlyFind recurring savings while checking service outcomes.+

Ask which costs can be reduced, by how much each year, and what changes for residents. Separate a lasting saving from a one-time reduction or deferred maintenance.

Start with service spending ↗
02Share or consolidate services where it truly savesCount net savings after transition costs and service impacts.+

Test joint delivery against the actual cost of providing the service. Moving a charge between funds or governments does not, by itself, save money. No savings estimate is established here.

Read the existing shared-cost explanation ↗
03Reconsider projects that remain uncommittedVerify contracts and obligations before counting any saving.+

Ask what can still change in scope or timing, what it would cost to change it, and what the community would give up. The project detail identifies the evidence gaps; it does not establish that a project can be cancelled.

Review project decisions and remaining questions →
04Examine development that adds more than it costsCompare lasting revenue with infrastructure and service costs.+

Test whether new taxable value would produce a net fiscal benefit after added costs and timing are considered. More development is not automatically more money available for existing services.

Start with the tax-base evidence ↗
05Use available savings during the transitionUse a portion of available savings to ease near-term tax pressure while longer-term spending and revenue changes take effect.+

Savings are finite. Restrictions, reserve needs and existing commitments must be checked before deciding how much, if any, is available. This can provide temporary help; it does not create recurring revenue.

Your input is the beginning. Our aim is to bring informed community priorities into Hillsborough’s budget conversations, then follow the decisions that shape our town.

  1. 01

    Understand the themes

    As participation grows, we aim to identify common themes, differing views and the tradeoffs readers raise.

  2. 02

    Bring priorities into the conversation

    Where possible, bring informed priorities and tradeoffs to Town leadership before budget decisions.

  3. 03

    Follow what happens

    Track documented responses and decisions, alongside questions that remain unresolved.
