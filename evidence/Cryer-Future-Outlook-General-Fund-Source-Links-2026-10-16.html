Outlook, debt & savings

Understand the outlook here. Follow the source links or open the detailed tables when you want to check a figure.

The Big Picture shows an annual General Fund gap of $18.8M in FY2039 if this scenario unfolds. Here is the detail behind it: what grows, which projects create payments, and what savings might cover. FY2030–FY2039 is a scenario, not an adopted Town budget.

What drives spending?

Personnel and operating costs, including fire protection, account for about 93% of the increase in annual net spending from FY2029 to FY2039 in this scenario.

Of the $19.2 million increase, personnel adds about $9.9 million, other modeled operating costs $6.5 million, and fire protection $1.4 million. These figures account for the relevant utility reimbursements.

The scenario starts with FY2029 values in the Town’s recommended plan and applies these assumptions through FY2039:

Personnel 7.30% a year
Based on compound growth in audited personnel spending from FY2018 to FY2025.
Selected operating costs 8.92% a year
Based on the same historical period, with identified FY2025 disaster costs removed. Fire protection and several separately modeled items are excluded from this growth calculation.
Orange Rural Fire 5% a year
Continues the increases in the Town’s FY2027–FY2029 recommended plan. Future funding remains subject to Town decisions.
Capital purchases 3% a year
A modeling allowance applied to FY2029 purchases, without a separately verified forecast basis.

Funding grows more slowly

Property-tax receipts grow 2% a year, rounding the Town plan’s approximately 1.81% annual growth from FY2026 to FY2029. This is growth in revenue collected, not a 2% tax-rate increase. Most other receipts and incoming transfers stay at FY2029 dollar levels. Continuing these assumptions after FY2029 is not a guarantee.

Historical growth is not inflation alone. It includes staffing, benefits, service changes, contracts and one-time costs, and can be affected by reporting changes. Extending those rates does not establish what future spending will be. FY2030–FY2039 is a scenario, not an adopted Town budget.

Scheduled loan payments, project contributions, reserve transfers and utility reimbursements remain in the model. Set-asides move money into project or reserve funds for current or future use. Annual spending includes cash contributions and loan repayments, not the full budgets of every project. Funding gaps are before additional use of savings.

Scope and limitations. The historical comparison uses gross General Fund personnel and broad operating categories before utility reimbursements, excluding capital, debt, transfers and separately reported Restricted Revenue Fund activity. Staffing, services, benefits, contracts, grants, irregular costs and reporting changes can affect the rates. Unidentified one-time costs may remain, and historical program-scope differences have not been fully normalized. No matched-period inflation benchmark is established here.

Which projects create loan payments?

Loans and timing. The Town’s debt schedule separates loans already taken out (“Existing loans”) from planned borrowing (“Future loans”). A–E identify the five future loans and mark when payments begin. These are payment dates, not construction start dates. FY2025 and FY2026 show total debt payments; loan-by-loan details begin in FY2027. The chart includes only the loans in that schedule.

What can residents still influence?

We cannot conclude that all five projects are beyond residents’ influence. Residents can ask the Town Board to reconsider scope, timing or future funding. Public feedback does not automatically stop a project; contracts and funding conditions may limit choices or make changes costly.

What is the next decision, who makes it, and when can residents give input before it is made? Ask for spending to date, remaining signed commitments, available alternatives, and the net cost of delay or stopping—including repayment or cancellation costs.

A projected fund balance is not the same as freely available cash. These figures do not establish how much could be spent or when savings might run out.

How are the reserve target and potential cushion estimated?

Reserve target. The Town’s policy sets a reserve target range of 33%–100% of annual General Fund operating expenses. The 33% figure is the lower end of that range. This preview estimates it at $7.2M by taking 33% of the $21.9M expense total used in the Town’s FY2029 financial summary. That total includes operating costs, debt payments, capital purchases and transfers to other funds, less utility reimbursements. Source: FY2027 Recommended Budget, pp. 18 and 20.

Potential cushion. The Town projects a General Fund balance of $11.8M at the end of FY2029. We still need to confirm how much of that balance is uncommitted and available under the reserve policy. The $4.6M above the estimated reserve target is therefore a potential cushion; it is not a confirmed amount available to spend.

The Cryer v52 model extends these figures using the assumptions shown above. Utility reimbursements are deducted from both funding and spending. Debt payments are included in spending.

Who shares the Town tax base?

A Hillsborough address does not by itself put a property inside Town limits. The saved Town records distinguish 4,124 with Town tax billed, 183 verified fully exempt, and 165 unresolved or other records.
