How to read these numbers

Personnel means pay and related benefits. It is not the full cost of a department: supplies, contracts, equipment and other spending sit outside these totals.

FTE means full-time equivalent: two half-time positions equal one FTE. These records describe authorized or allocated positions, not average filled headcount.

History and plan use different source editions. Historical audit figures are not silently replaced by later budget-book figures. Recommended budgets are proposals, not confirmed adopted budgets.

About the inflation line

The teal reference asks what the first displayed historical personnel amount would be if it increased only with general consumer prices. It is not the Town’s actual spending or a recommended budget.

We use the Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI-U, U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted (series CUUR0000SA0). Each fiscal-year index is the average of its 12 monthly readings, July through June, matching the Town’s fiscal year. Reference dollars = base-year personnel dollars × current fiscal-year CPI ÷ base fiscal-year CPI.

For example, average consumer prices rose 28.1% from FY2018 to FY2025. The reference uses a department’s own first displayed historical year, which may differ. CPI measures household prices, not the Town’s particular wages, benefits or service mix. A difference from this line does not by itself show waste, efficiency or a staffing change.

The historical CPI reference ends with displayed audit spending. The separate plan-panel scenario starts afresh at FY2025 appendix actual, then increases that amount by an assumed 3% annually: base × 1.03 raised to the number of years since FY2025. Four years compound to 12.550881%, displayed as 12.55%. It does not carry an audit baseline across a different appendix basis. We omit comparisons where known scope transitions prevent a useful reading.

Why 3%? CPI averaged 299.685 in FY2023 and 317.731 in FY2025. That is a 6.021656% cumulative increase, or 2.966818% annualized over two years, rounded to 3% for this illustrative scenario. It is neither the current inflation rate nor a prediction. The scenario is hypothetical for all four years, including FY2026; October 2025 CPI is unavailable, so we have not substituted an incomplete FY2026 average for a full fiscal year. CPI measures household prices, not the precise costs of municipal staffing.
